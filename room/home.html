Cut the cardboard out of a game, once

Scans of a board game's component sheets go in. Out come the counters, cards, tiles and terrain as separate pictures at their real printed size, named, and checked off against what the box should contain.

  1. ImportDrop a PDF, a scan, a Word file with pictures in it, or a ZIP. Every page becomes a sheet.
  2. OutlineDraw round each piece once — or take the outlines the tool suggests, which on cards and counters is usually all of them.
  3. CutOne press a sheet. Each piece comes out at full resolution, edge smoothed, measured in inches.
  4. NameSay what each piece is, by dragging its name from your checklist onto it.
  5. CheckSee what is still to cut — by set, by kind, by how far you have got.

Everything stays on this machine. The room listens on 127.0.0.1 only and fetches nothing from anywhere.

Where do the files come from?
A PDF of component sheets — every page becomes a sheet. A picture of a scan — PNG, JPEG or TIFF. A Word file with the pictures inside it, which is how a good many fan-made card sets are shared. A ZIP of any of those, opened and imported in one go.
⚠️ A whole folder in Google Drive or Dropbox: use their own Download — it hands you a ZIP — and drop that here. A single file can be pasted in as a link instead, once it is inside a project, if it is shared.
