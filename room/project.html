Keep score of what is still to cut — if you want to

A checklist is the game's own contents list: every counter, card, tile and template the box should have. The room then tells you which ones you have cut and which you have not, and how far through you are.

It is optional and it can wait. Cutting works perfectly well without one.

A line each. A number at either end becomes the quantity:
26 Damage counters · Turning template x2 · Long range ruler · 9 | Large templates | template

— 0 of 0 components accounted for — 0 cut and linked, 0 probably cut (a piece's name matches), 0 part of a deck, 0 not yet.

ComponentSetKindQtyWhereStatusPieces

Status is worked out from the pieces: a piece linked to a component (on Match, or on the Pieces page) counts as cut; otherwise a piece whose name matches the component's pattern counts as probably cut. Edit a name or quantity in place; it is saved when you leave the box.
⭐️ One is enough under the quantity means what the room has always assumed: a sheet printing twenty-six identical damage counters wants one cut, because the game repeats it. Press it to say all different — a deck of twenty-four damage cards is twenty-four different pieces of card, and the line then reads 3 of 24 until every one is cut. Nothing in a printed contents list can tell those two apart, so it is yours to say.

Take the pieces out of the room

The room writes a plain folder that anything can read: every piece as an ordinary picture named by what it is, at its true printed size, with a list beside it as a spreadsheet and as data. Nothing in it is shaped for any particular program.

Check the cut against the contents list

Read this before the pieces leave the room. It says what is missing, what is half-done, and — the part nothing else can tell you — which cut pieces answer to nothing on the list. It only reports: nothing here changes a piece or a component.

It is for your own use, with a copy of the game you own — replacing a piece you have lost, playing a game you already have, keeping a record of what is on your own shelf. Do not put these pieces on the internet, share them, sell them, or build them into anything you release.

The project

If something goes wrong

Three things in a project cannot be rebuilt from the scans: the outlines you drew, the names you gave the pieces, and the checklist. So the room keeps the last 60 copies of each of them, by itself, every time one is saved. You do not have to do anything for this to happen.

Kept in . Putting a copy back also keeps the one it replaces, so it is not a one-way door either. ⚠️ Close the cutting table first if you are putting outlines back — a table left open will save what is on its screen over the top.
